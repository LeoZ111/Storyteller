# Chapter 12: Cracks

On the evening before the gathering, there was meat.

That was unusual enough that Chil announced it three times before it reached the table. Dal had bought a chicken with the twelve nyang from Wolgok, a scrawny, tough chicken with legs like shoelaces, and boiled it with ginger and garlic until the meat fell off the bones. The broth smelled through the whole house. Even the Master had put her pipe down.

Do-yun told them about the gorge. He told it badly, as always, and left out the most important part, as always. He didn't mention the saber that broke into four pieces. He said one of the bandits had been coughing and he hoped the man would see a doctor. Chil wanted to know how many there had been. Do-yun said a few.

Mok Yeon saw the Master watching the First Disciple over the rim of her bowl. She heard something in him. Maybe the hum that still lingered a little in his right hand.

Then Seol-a set down her chopsticks.

They clicked. One clean note, like on the first morning. But this time everyone stopped eating.

[Yu Seol-a|calm, controlled] Master. I have something to say.

The Master took a sip of broth. She set the bowl down.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|calm] Say it.

[Yu Seol-a|very straight] The Cheon family has offered me a place as a guest disciple. In their hall in Seoan. With teachers, a library, and a training ground where the rain doesn't come in. I've accepted. I leave on the third day of the eighth month.

It was very quiet. Outside the crickets chirped. In the kitchen Dal rattled a lid and then stopped.

Chil stared at Seol-a, mouth half open, a piece of chicken halfway between bowl and lips. Do-yun had lowered his head. His big hands lay flat on the table on either side of his bowl, like someone who needs to hold on.

The Master said nothing.

[Yu Seol-a|faster, as if afraid of being interrupted] I know what it means. I know what people will say. That I'm running when it gets hard. That the only gifted one is leaving the sinking ship. But I'm twenty, Master. I've practiced on this mountain for eleven years, on a ground with broken posts, with a sword whose tassel I have to dye myself. I beat three disciples of the Iron Gate, and people laughed because they thought it was luck. It wasn't luck.

She drew a breath.

[Yu Seol-a|quieter] I don't want to grow old here ringing a bell nobody hears.

Mok Yeon sat very still. He felt Chil start to tremble beside him.

[Bang Chil|voice cracking] But we have two bells now.

Seol-a looked at him. For a moment her face softened. Then it didn't.

[Yu Seol-a|quietly] Two out of nine, Chil.

Chil dropped the chicken. It plopped into the broth. He stood up so fast his stool fell over and ran out, and this time he didn't use the Sparrow Step. You could hear every single footfall across the courtyard to the woodpile.

***

The Master waited until it was quiet outside. Then she looked at Seol-a for a long time, with that gaze that listened.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|calm] Go.

Seol-a blinked.

[Yu Seol-a|unsure] Master?

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|calm] You heard me. Go to Seoan. Learn from their teachers. Read their books. Practice on a ground where the rain doesn't come in.

Something shifted in Seol-a's face. Mok Yeon saw it and understood, because he had felt it himself once. She had prepared for a fight. She had readied the words, the reasons, the defense, the way she prepared for every duel, and now she stood there with her sword raised and no one to raise it against.

[Yu Seol-a|bitter, suddenly loud] That's all? Go? Eleven years, and you say go as if I'd asked whether I could fetch salt?

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|unmoved] What else should I say?

[Yu Seol-a|trembling] You could say you need me. You could say I should stay. You could at least pretend it matters to you!

The Master picked her pipe up off the table. She turned it between her fingers. She didn't light it.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|very quietly] I've watched eleven disciples leave, Seol-a. I asked every one of them to stay. Every single one. Four stayed because I asked, and of those four, one hated me later, and one died of a fever he would have survived in Seoan, where there are doctors. I don't ask anyone to stay anymore. Whoever stays should stay because they want to.

Seol-a stood. Her face was white.

[Yu Seol-a|flat] Then I don't.

She bowed exactly as deeply as was proper, not a hand's width deeper, and left.

Do-yun stood as well, heavily, and went out to look for Chil. He said nothing. In passing he rested his hand briefly on the Master's shoulder, and she allowed it.

Then only the Master and Mok Yeon remained at the table for twenty. The broth went cold. The chicken floated in Chil's bowl.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|without looking up] You didn't say anything.

[Mok Yeon|quietly] No.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|dry] You knew.

He didn't answer. She heard what his heart was doing anyway.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|after a while] Tomorrow is the seventh day. You haven't rung the bell properly yet.

[Mok Yeon|calm] Tomorrow morning.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|standing] Then sleep. If you ring it badly tomorrow, I go alone.

She left. Mok Yeon stayed sitting, looking at the cold chicken, and after a while Dal came out of the kitchen, cleared the bowls, and set a bowl of rice in front of him. With salt.

***

He found Seol-a on the roof of the main hall.

He hadn't been looking for her. He couldn't sleep and had gone outside, and then he heard her. Not with Bell Ear. Just like that. The soft scrape of cloth on tiles. She was sitting on the ridge, right where the two tiles were missing, knees drawn up, looking north. Toward Seoan.

He climbed up over the kitchen awning. It took a long time and his ribs complained, and when he reached the top she looked at him as if he were a raccoon that had gotten lost on the roof.

[Yu Seol-a|cold] What do you want?

[Mok Yeon|sitting down, at a distance] Nothing.

She turned back to the north. For a long time neither of them said anything. Down in the valley lay the mist. Above them stood the moon, nearly full.

[Yu Seol-a|finally, without looking at him] You're not going to tell me to stay either.

[Mok Yeon|calm] No.

[Yu Seol-a|sharp] Why not? You tell everyone else what they're doing wrong. My fourth step. Do-yun's wagon. You walk around like an old man who's seen everything before.

He thought about what he could say. The truth wouldn't do. That in his first life she had left and survived because she left. That for forty-one years he hadn't known what became of her, and that now he couldn't decide which was worse: letting her go, or making her stay.

[Mok Yeon|slowly] Because you're right. You're better than this mountain. You should have teachers. And a ground where the rain doesn't come in.

She slowly turned her head. She had obviously expected anything but that.

[Mok Yeon|continuing, quietly] But when you're there. In Seoan. Listen closely.

[Yu Seol-a|suspicious] To what?

[Mok Yeon|after a pause] To everything. To the people. To their voices when they're being kind. You're almost at the Fourth Tone. I can hear it in your sword. Soon you'll hear when someone lies. When that happens, don't look away. Even if it's someone you like.

She looked at him. In the moonlight her face was very pale and very young, and for the first time she didn't look cold, only confused.

[Yu Seol-a|quietly] You talk as if you knew something about the Cheon family.

He didn't answer.

[Yu Seol-a|earnest] Mok Yeon. Ui-jin is a good person. I've heard him when he talks. His heart is the same as his voice. There's nothing underneath.

[Mok Yeon|earnest] I know. I heard him too.

That was true. He'd seen it in the market: the dirty hands, the unguarded smile. A heart without an undertone.

A heart that doesn't yet know who his father is, he thought. He didn't know much more himself. Only what he had gathered about Cheon Mu-gyeong over forty-one years, fragment by fragment, and every one of those fragments had been something good. A hero. A savior. A father of refugees. That was the worst part of all.

[Yu Seol-a|standing, brushing off her trousers] You're strange. Ever since the morning with the bell, you've been strange. Chil says you're a ghost.

[Mok Yeon|dry] Chil feeds me.

She made a small sound that was almost a laugh. Then she climbed down off the roof, light and sure, much faster than he had, and halfway down she stopped once more.

[Yu Seol-a|without turning around] Ring the bell properly tomorrow. The Master wants to take you. I heard it. She wants it more than she admits.

Then she was gone.

***

He didn't sleep.

He sat beneath the bell called Dawn all night, back against the beam, looking up at it, and beside it hung Dew, slender and green, and above them hung seven empty hooks.

He had rung the morning bell eighteen times since he woke. Each time a little better. Each time wrong. A splinter of unrest at the end of the note. He had tried striking it harder. He had tried striking it more gently. He had tried holding his breath differently, gripping the rope differently, standing differently. It wasn't his hands. He knew how to strike a bell. For forty years he had struck stones, trees, and cliff faces as if they were bells.

He looked up at the founder's four characters on the rim. Half worn away. Listen before you strike.

He had read them a thousand times. He had always taken them as a rule of combat. Hear your opponent before you strike. Hear his intent. Bell Ear before Bell Strike.

He thought about how he had rung the bell as a boy. Hastily, afraid, because the Master was waiting below and everyone was listening and he would get it wrong again. He thought about how he had been ringing it since he woke. With everything he had. With forty-one years. With the rain and the steps and the night in the bronze. Every strike had been a promise, an apology, a proof.

He had never listened to what the bell wanted.

He stood. He laid his palm flat on the bronze, on the pale patch, and closed his eyes.

The bell wasn't silent. A bell is never quite silent. The wind passed through the tower and touched it, and it answered with a hum so faint it was more a feeling than a sound. Beneath his palm it swayed, slowly, in a measure that was its own and not his. He listened. He listened the way you listen to someone who hasn't spoken in a very long time. He heard the age in the bronze, the notch from the sword, the places where it was thinner and the places where it was thicker. He heard where it wanted to breathe.

Outside, the sky turned grey.

He kept his hand there until he no longer knew where his skin ended and the bronze began. Then he reached for the rope.

He didn't breathe in nine times. He waited. He waited until the bell, moved by the wind, had swung out completely once and came to rest for a single heartbeat, at that one point where a bell is neither coming nor going.

And then he pulled. Not hard. Not gently. Exactly the way it wanted.

The tone came.

It was deep and full and warm, and at the end it had no splinter. It had no end at all. It simply went on, out of the tower, across the courtyard, over the forty-seven steps, into the mist that was turning pink in the first light, and Dew beside it hummed along without anyone touching it, very softly, like a younger sister singing along in her sleep.

Mok Yeon stood beneath the bell and didn't cry. He only stood there and listened, until the tone had grown so faint he no longer knew whether he was still hearing it or only remembering it.

Down in the courtyard, a door opened.

The Master stepped out. She was in her sleeping robe, her hair loose, grey and long, and he had never seen her like this, not in this life and not in the other. She stood in the middle of the courtyard, barefoot among the moss and dandelions, and looked up at the tower.

For a long time she said nothing.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|rough, barely audible] Ryu Gang used to ring it like that.

Then she cleared her throat, and when she spoke again, she was the Master.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|curt, loud] Get dressed. Something decent. We're going to Hacheon.
