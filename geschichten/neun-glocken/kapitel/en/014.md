# Chapter 14: I've Been Here Before

They didn't speak on the way home.

The Master walked ahead, up the forty-seven steps, so fast that Mok Yeon with his stitched ribs could barely keep up. She didn't look back once. In the courtyard Chil came running to meet them, wanting to know if they'd gotten contracts, if the Patriarch had been nice, if there had been cake, and the Master walked past him as if he were air. Chil stopped, mouth open. Then he looked at Mok Yeon.

[Bang Chil|whispering] What did you do?

[Mok Yeon|quietly] Spilled tea.

[Bang Chil|horrified] On who?

Mok Yeon didn't answer. He kept walking, toward the main hall, where the Master had disappeared. The door stood open.

She wasn't in the hall. He heard her above, in the tower; the ladder creaked under her steps.

***

She was sitting beneath Dew, her back against a beam, right where he had sat the night before. The pipe lay beside her on the boards. Unlit.

He sat beneath Dawn. Between them hung the two bells, green and still, and above them the seven empty hooks. The sun was already low. Red light fell through the open sides of the tower and painted long shadows across the floor.

For a long time neither of them said anything.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|finally, flat] When he smiled.

Mok Yeon waited.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|slowly, as if searching for words for something that has none] His voice is true. That's the terrible part. He means what he says. The children, the grass, the camps. Every word. I listened very closely, and there was nothing false in his voice. But when he smiled, he moved his qi. Just a little. And underneath…

She broke off. She picked up the pipe, turned it, set it down again.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|very quietly] It sounded like people screaming inside him. Very far away. Many of them. As if behind a thick door. I've never heard anything like it, Mok Yeon. Not from bandits. Not from murderers. I've heard men who sold their own children, and they didn't sound like that.

[Mok Yeon|quietly] I heard a second heartbeat. When he helped me up. Beneath his own.

She looked at him. In the red light her face was very old.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|after a long pause] There are old stories. Ryu Gang told them to me when we were young, to scare me. About arts that don't strengthen your own qi but take someone else's. You lay your hand on a person and drink them out like a well. The old ones called it Blood Marrow. Whoever does it carries the others inside them. Their heartbeats. Their voices. Until they fall silent. The Murim Alliance banned it a hundred years ago. Whoever practices it is executed, without trial.

She stood. She did it abruptly, with a jerk, as if she had decided.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|firm] I have to ask him.

[Mok Yeon|calm] No.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|sharp] I have to. A man who sounds like that can't be the man who talks like him. Maybe it's an illness. Maybe he doesn't know himself. Maybe it's something I don't understand. I'm fifty-eight years old, Mok Yeon, and I don't accuse anyone without asking them first. I'll go down to Hacheon tomorrow morning and ask to speak with him, and then…

[Mok Yeon|completely calm] And then, on the night of the Mid-Autumn Festival, men with black lanterns come up the mountain. They come through the side gate and through the main gate. Do-yun holds the gate until he stops roaring. Chil cries, very briefly. And you stand in the courtyard calling my name.

The Master stood perfectly still.

He looked at her. For forty-one years he had wondered how he would say it, if he could ever tell anyone. He had gathered words like stones. Now they were all gone, and there was only the truth, and it was very simple.

[Mok Yeon|quietly] And I hide in the bell.

***

She sat back down. Slowly.

He told her everything.

He didn't tell it well. He had never been a good storyteller, and forty-one years of solitude hadn't improved it. He told it the way Do-yun told stories, with the high points in the wrong places. He told her about the night in the bronze, about the morning after, the courtyard black and wet. He told her about the years after, about caves and peaks and a prison in Mujang. About two pages of notes from which he had pieced together an art over forty years. About the name he had found after twenty years of searching and believed after twenty more. About the hundred and eight steps in the rain, a strike after which no sound came, and an old man in white silk who said: You only had to ask, boy.

And then about waking. About the rope in his hands. About a note that hadn't been right.

The Master didn't interrupt him once.

He knew she was listening. Not to the words. Beneath them. Through the whole long, badly told account, her Undertone rested on him like a hand on a bell, and he felt her test every sentence, every word, every pause.

She found nothing.

When he finished, the sun had set. The tower lay in darkness; only a narrow violet strip remained in the west. Down in the courtyard a lantern was burning. Dal called them to eat. No one answered him.

The Master sat for a very long time without saying anything.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|finally, rough] How did I die?

[Mok Yeon|after a pause] At the gate. Beside Do-yun. You were calling my name. You were shouting for me to hide.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|quietly] And you hid.

[Mok Yeon|barely audible] Yes.

She breathed out. A long breath. Then she reached for the pipe, took a flint from her belt, struck it, and the small red point glowed in the dark. She drew on it. The smell of tobacco settled between them, between the two bells.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|dry, after the first draw] Good.

He blinked.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|calm] I told you to hide. And you hid. That was the only time in six years you did what I said without whining.

He stared at her. She wasn't looking at him. She was looking up into the dark beams, her face only half visible in the pipe's glow.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|quieter] Mok Yeon. A sixteen-year-old boy who hides when murderers come isn't a coward. He's a sixteen-year-old boy. I didn't call you so you would fight. I called you so you would live. And you lived. Forty-one years. You kept going with those two pages Ryu Gang stared at for twenty years, and you reached the Eighth Tone, alone, without a teacher, without a bell.

Her voice went very rough.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|almost toneless] Do you know how many masters of the Nine Bells Gate have reached the Eighth Tone in a hundred and forty years? One. The founder.

He couldn't say anything. Something in his chest that had been shut for forty-one years like a bell under a tarp gave off a note he didn't recognize.

He didn't cry. He only sat there beneath Dawn and held on to his knees with both hands, and the Master smoked and looked into the beams and let him.

***

Later, after Dal had stopped calling and Chil had at some point climbed the ladder with a bowl of rice, set it on the top rung, and climbed back down without a word, they talked about what had to be done.

It was strange, planning with her. For forty years he had planned alone.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|matter-of-fact] Flee?

[Mok Yeon|shaking his head] Where to? The lanterns are watching us. If we flee, he'll know you heard something. Then they'll hunt us. And the bells stay here.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|nodding] Accuse him?

[Mok Yeon|calm] Before whom? The Murim Alliance? With what? With what an impoverished sect leader claims she heard and what a boy who runs from dogs claims he dreamed? Today two hundred people cheered him. Tomorrow he gives rice to three thousand refugees.

She drew on the pipe. The glow brightened and dimmed.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|slowly] So we pretend I heard nothing.

[Mok Yeon|nodding] You don't go to him. You act as always. You're angry with me about the tea. Everyone should see it. If he sends people to ask questions, you're an old woman ashamed of her stupid disciple.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|dry] That won't be hard.

[Mok Yeon|continuing] And meanwhile we get the mountain ready. The west wall. The side gate. Traps on the goat path. And the bells.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|attentive] The bells?

[Mok Yeon|after a pause] In my first life I didn't have any. I didn't know what two bells could do together. Three days ago I heard it. A third tone, out of the air between them. If we have bells in the right places, at the wall, at the gate, on the path, we'll hear them coming long before they arrive. And maybe more than that.

She looked at him. He saw her think it over, turn it in her mind the way she turned the pipe between her fingers.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|after a while] Seol-a.

He had known that was coming.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|hard] She's going into his house. In four weeks. I won't let my disciple go under the roof of a man who sounds like that.

[Mok Yeon|quietly] In my first life she went. And she survived. Because she wasn't here.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|sharp] And what became of her then?

He was silent.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|quieter, bitter] You don't know.

[Mok Yeon|honest] No. They called her the Frost Blossom Blade. She fought for the Cheon family. Then I heard nothing more.

For a long time the Master said nothing at all. The pipe had gone out. She didn't relight it.

[Mok Yeon|carefully] If you forbid her, she'll want to know why. She won't obey. Not now, not after last night. She'll go to Ui-jin and tell him her master has something against his family. And Ui-jin will tell his father. He can't hide anything. He never learned how.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|barely audible] And if we tell her? The truth?

[Mok Yeon|after a long pause] Then she won't believe us. Or she'll believe us and go anyway, to hear it for herself. She's like you.

The Master laughed. Short and ugly, without any joy.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|rough] Yes. That's her.

She put the pipe down. She drew her knees up the way Seol-a had on the roof and wrapped her arms around them, and for a moment she didn't look like a sect leader at all. She looked like a woman who has just learned she will die in sixty-eight days and now has to choose which of her children to send into which fire.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|after a long time] We let her go. For now. But not blind. We find a way to stay in contact with her. And if she hears something in Seoan, and she will hear something, that girl has ears like a bat, then she should know where she can run.

[Mok Yeon|nodding] Yes.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|after a pause] And Do-yun?

[Mok Yeon|slowly] Soon. He has to know before we start building traps. Otherwise he'll ask. He asked me two days ago who I am.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|dry] And what did you say?

[Mok Yeon|earnest] His junior brother.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|after a moment] Well, that's true.

***

It was late when they climbed down the ladder.

The courtyard was dark. Light still burned in the kitchen, and through the window you could see Dal sitting by the stove, dozing, his head against the wall, the long spoon still in his hand. On the bottom rung stood a second bowl of rice, cold, with salt, and beside it, wrapped in a cabbage leaf, half a dried fish.

The Master looked at the bowl. Then at Mok Yeon.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|dry] Forty-one years. And you still eat rice with salt.

[Mok Yeon|earnest] It keeps forever.

She snorted. It was almost a laugh. Then she stopped in the middle of the courtyard and looked up at the tower once more, at the two bells hanging in the moonlight, green and still.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|quietly] Mok Yeon. How many days?

He didn't have to count. He had counted every morning since he'd held the rope in his hands.

[Mok Yeon|calm] Sixty-eight.

She nodded. She said nothing more. She went to her room, and at the door she stopped once more, without turning around.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|barely audible] This time you don't hide. And this time I don't call.

The door closed.

Mok Yeon stayed alone in the courtyard. He sat down on the bottom rung of the ladder, took the cold bowl of rice and the half fish and ate both, slowly, and above him in the tower Dawn and Dew hummed very softly in the night wind, and for the first time in forty-one years he had the feeling that someone was listening alongside him.
