# Chapter 1: The Bell I Hid In

It was raining on the steps of the Murim Alliance Hall, and each of the hundred and eight steps sounded different.

Mok Yeon could hear it, though he was lying on his back. The upper steps, polished smooth by the boots of grandmasters, gave a thin, bright note. The lower ones were cracked; water gathered in the grooves there and gurgled. His own blood ran down the steps, warm and slow, and even that had a sound, if you listened closely enough. For forty-one years he had taught himself to listen closely.

Above, beneath the sweeping eaves, stood an old man in white silk, hands tucked into his sleeves as if nothing had happened. Cheon Mu-gyeong. The Righteous Blade. Seventy-nine years old, Honored Elder of the Alliance, savior of Seoan, father of refugees. Around him stood the masters of the Hundred Sects, looking down at the man in the rain who had just attacked him.

A madman. A hermit without a sect. The one the mountain villages called the Bell Ghost.

[Cheon Mu-gyeong|calm, almost pitying] You only had to ask, boy. I would have listened.

Mok Yeon laughed, and blood came up with it. Boy. He was fifty-seven. But maybe the old man was right. Maybe he had never grown older than sixteen.

He had practiced the strike for forty-one years. A single strike, meant to pass through a man's qi and make the false note inside it audible to everyone. He had landed it, right beneath the breastbone. And the sound that should have followed never came. Instead Cheon Mu-gyeong had raised a hand, almost gently, and Mok Yeon's meridians had snapped like old strings.

The masters saw only an old man defending himself against a lunatic.

No one had heard it.

No one ever listened.

The rain grew louder. Mok Yeon closed his eyes, and behind his lids it wasn't dark. It was the color of bronze.

***

The inside of a bell smells of copper and old grease.

He had been sixteen, knees pulled up to his chin, back pressed against the cold metal. The bell was large enough for a skinny boy to disappear into, if he made himself small enough. He had made himself very small.

Outside, the lanterns were burning.

It had been the night of the Mid-Autumn Festival, and the men had come up the mountain carrying black paper lanterns, silent as fog. Through the bronze he heard everything. Bronze carries sound; it doesn't swallow it. He heard the gate break. He heard Han Do-yun roar, deep and long like an ox, and then not anymore. He heard the Sect Leader call a name, his name, Mok Yeon, Mok Yeon, where are you, hide. And he had hidden. He had done exactly what she told him to, and he had not done it for her sake.

He heard little Chil cry, very briefly.

After that, for a long time, he heard only paper crackling in the wind.

In the morning he crawled out. He was the only one.

***

A sound woke him.

No rain. No blood. A deep, full tone that passed through his ribs as though someone had wrapped a warm cloth around his heart, and that faded slowly while birds took flight below him.

Mok Yeon opened his eyes.

Mist. White, drifting mist, through which the first light of morning fell in slanted beams, golden and cold at once. Wooden beams above him, dark with age, a swallow in its nest. And in front of him, so close that he could see his own shape in it, warped and greenish: a bell.

He knew every flaw in that bronze. The notch in the rim where a sword had struck it a hundred years ago. The founder's four characters, half worn away: Listen before you strike. The patch where the patina was lighter, because generations of disciples had secretly leaned their foreheads against the metal there to feel it hum.

The bell called Dawn. The last bell of the Nine Bells Gate.

He had last seen it forty-one years ago, when he crawled out of it.

There was a rope in his hands. Rough hemp, frayed at one end. He stared at the hands holding it. Narrow hands. Too smooth. No scars on the knuckles, no crooked fingers, no missing fingertip on the left little finger. The hands of a boy who had never carried anything heavy.

[Mok Yeon|thoughts] Is this death? Then death is very petty.

Because the sound that had woken him had been his own. He must have pulled the rope in his sleep, or while dying, or in some space between that had no name. The bell was still humming, and he could hear it clearly: the note had been good, but not right. Struck a breath too hard. A splinter of unrest at the end.

As a boy, he had never once rung the morning bell correctly. Not a single time.

With trembling fingers he tightened his grip on the rope, drew a breath, and pulled.

It was a terrible strike. He put everything he had into it, forty-one years, the rain, the steps, and the body that obeyed him was so weak the clapper hit crooked. The bell screamed. A hoarse, clanging sound rolled down the mountain, and somewhere in the valley a dog started barking.

Below him, in the courtyard, something clattered to the ground.

[Bang Chil|shrieking, outraged] Mok Yeon! Have you lost your mind? That was horrible! The Master's going to kill you!

Mok Yeon froze.

He knew that voice. High, a little hoarse, right in the middle of breaking, so it cracked on every third word. He hadn't heard it in forty-one years, except on the nights he couldn't sleep. On those nights it had only ever made one sound. Very brief.

He threw himself down the bell tower ladder, missed two rungs, banged his knee, and barely felt it.

In the courtyard stood a boy of thirteen, round as a rice dumpling, in a grey-blue robe too short for him so that his ankles stuck out. An overturned wooden bucket lay at his feet, water trickling between the paving stones. He had a runny nose, a mole beneath his left eye, and half a steamed bun clutched in his fist that he had very obviously stolen.

Bang Chil.

Alive. Chubby-cheeked. Indignant.

Mok Yeon stopped three steps away from him and suddenly didn't know what to do with his arms.

[Bang Chil|suspicious] What are you staring at? I didn't steal the bun. It was just lying there. It was basically an orphan.

Mok Yeon took two steps and hugged him.

He did it clumsily, too hard, like someone who hadn't held another person in decades and had forgotten where the arms are supposed to go. Chil smelled of woodsmoke and sesame oil and boy. His heart beat fast and loud against Mok Yeon's chest, a little too fast, because he'd been startled. Mok Yeon could hear it. He could hear every single beat.

[Bang Chil|muffled, panicking] Let go! Let go, you're freezing! Are you possessed? Master! Mok Yeon's possessed! A bell ghost got him!

Mok Yeon let go, because the boy was squirming, and because he realized his face was wet.

Chil saw it too. He stopped squirming. His mouth hung open, the half bun forgotten in midair.

[Bang Chil|quietly, unsure] You're crying. Hey. Did you hurt yourself that bad on the ladder?

[Mok Yeon|hoarse, slow] Yes. On the ladder.

[Bang Chil|after a pause, magnanimous] Want half?

He held out the bun. Half of the half. Mok Yeon took it, because he didn't know what else to do, and bit into it. It was cold and chewy and tasted of nothing at all, and it was the best thing he had ever eaten.

***

The Nine Bells Gate was not a great sect. It wasn't even a middling one.

In the morning light, as the mist slowly lifted from the roofs, Mok Yeon saw all of it again for the first time. Five buildings around a courtyard of cracked flagstones, with moss and dandelions growing from the seams. The main hall with its sagging ridge, missing two tiles, so that when it rained it dripped onto the altar. The kitchen, thin smoke already rising from its chimney. The training ground with its battered wooden posts. And behind it all the bell tower, narrow and too tall for the other buildings, like a thin neck straining toward something.

Nine hooks hung from the beams of the tower. Eight of them were empty.

He had heard this place burn. And seen it afterward, in the morning, black and wet. Every year on the fifteenth day of the ninth month he had lit a lantern, wherever he happened to be, in caves, on peaks, in prisons, and every year he had forgotten a little more of what the main hall looked like inside. Now here it stood, with its crooked roof and its two missing tiles, and he thought that if he blinked, it would vanish.

He didn't blink.

Then the door of the main hall opened, and a woman stepped out.

She wasn't tall, but she stood in a way that made you remember her taller. A dark grey robe, carefully patched at the elbows. Iron-grey hair streaked with white, pinned in a severe knot pierced by a plain wooden hairpin. A narrow, sharp face with deep lines beside the mouth that came from frowning, not laughing, though she liked to laugh when no one was looking. Between her teeth was a long bamboo pipe, unlit.

Seo Hwa-ryeong. Sect Leader of the Nine Bells Gate.

The last time he had heard her, she was calling his name.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|dry, pipe between her teeth] Which one of you tried to murder our bell?

Chil instantly pointed at Mok Yeon. No hesitation, arm fully extended, the entire weight of his small body behind that finger.

Seo Hwa-ryeong took the pipe out of her mouth and looked at Mok Yeon. Her gaze was just as he remembered: patient in a way that made you more nervous than any impatience. She looked at him as if she were listening, though he wasn't saying anything.

And maybe she was. She had reached the Fourth Tone. He knew now what that meant. Back then, he hadn't.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|faintly mocking] Well? Did the bell do something to you? Or did you finally notice you have arms?

Mok Yeon dropped to his knees.

Not slowly, not solemnly. His legs simply gave out and he fell forward, forehead to the cold, damp flagstones, hands flat beside it, and he stayed there. The stones smelled of moss and rain. Somewhere behind him, Chil finally dropped the bun.

He wanted to say something. He'd had forty-one years to work out what he would say. I'm sorry. I ran. I heard you and I didn't come. I heard all of you.

What came out was:

[Mok Yeon|choked, barely audible] You're alive.

A long silence.

He heard her breathing. Calm, even, with a faint rattle at the end that came from too many years of the pipe. He heard her heartbeat give a small jump. Not from fear. From confusion.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|after a pause, entirely different, quiet] Chil. Get Dal. Tell him to boil ginger water. The boy has a fever.

[Bang Chil|whispering, awed] Or a ghost.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|sharp] Ginger water works on both. Run.

Chil ran. Mok Yeon heard his footsteps patter across the courtyard, light and quick, like a sparrow hopping over a roof. He had forgotten how lightly Chil ran. He had forgotten so much.

Then he felt a hand on the back of his head. Dry, calloused, warm. It stayed only a moment. It didn't stroke his hair. It simply rested there, the way you rest a hand on a bell to feel whether it's still ringing.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|gruff] Get up before you crack your skull on my stones. I can't afford a doctor.

He got up. His knees were shaking.

[Mok Yeon|hoarse] Master. What day is it?

She looked at him for a long time. Then she put the pipe back between her teeth.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|curt] The fifteenth of the sixth month. Taehwa twelve. Why? Did you forget how old you are, too?

The sixth month. The fifteenth day.

Mok Yeon counted. He counted the way he had counted heartbeats inside the bell to keep from going mad: slowly, one at a time.

The rest of the sixth month. The seventh. The eighth. The ninth, up to the fifteenth day, the night of the Mid-Autumn Festival, when the men with the black lanterns would come up the mountain.

Ninety days.

Above him in the tower, the bell called Dawn was still humming very softly, a murmur at the edge of hearing that no one but him noticed.

This time, he thought, I won't hide.
