# Chapter 24: The Inspector

Mok Yeon recognized her by her voice before he saw her.

She came up the forty-seven steps on the first day of the tenth month, just after sunrise, and she was talking to someone on the way. Not loudly. Matter-of-factly. In short, complete sentences, as if dictating to a clerk, which was exactly what she was doing.

[Baek Ha-seon|from the steps, matter-of-fact] Forty-two. Forty-three. Note: the steps are in poor condition. Several cracked. Fresh scrape marks on the thirty-fourth. Probably a sledge or a runner. Something heavy.

[Clerk|panting] Yes, Inspector.

[Baek Ha-seon|calm] And on the thirty-ninth, dried blood. Not fresh. Two weeks, perhaps more. Note it.

Mok Yeon stood at the gate and listened, and something in his chest tightened.

He knew that voice. He had last heard it twenty-three years ago. Not twenty-three years ago. Twenty-three years from now. In a prison in Mujang, through iron bars, when he was thirty-nine and had been arrested for murdering four men of the Black Lanterns.

Back then she had been the Chief Inspector of the Murim Alliance. Grey, gaunt, with a scar through her left eyebrow. She had been the only one who listened to him. All night. She had written down every word. And in the morning she let him go, because she had no proof that the four men weren't exactly what he said they were. Murderers.

She hadn't believed what he said about Cheon Mu-gyeong. But she had written it down.

***

Baek Ha-seon came through the gate.

She was thirty-four, tall and thin, with a long, serious face and black hair cut short, shorter than Do-yun's. The scar through her left eyebrow was already there. Fresh. Red. She wore a black robe without any ornament, only a small silver badge on the left breast: two crossed swords in a circle. At her hip hung no sword, but a wooden box in which Mok Yeon heard brushes rattle.

Behind her came the clerk, a slim young man with a bundle of paper under his arm, and an Alliance guard, broad, silent, with a saber on his back. And behind the guard, puffing, red-faced, with the black beard that looked combed down from his head onto his face: Master Pyeon.

Baek Ha-seon stopped in the middle of the courtyard and looked around.

Not the way Hwang Ryeom had looked around. Not like someone with a map in his head. Like someone with a blank page in her head, filling it. Her gaze passed over the new west wall. Over the side gate with its iron bolt. Over the notches in the wood of the main gate where sabers had bitten. Over the tower. Over the two bells. Over the little bells on their threads, ringing softly in the morning wind.

[Baek Ha-seon|without turning, to the clerk] Note: small bells. Many. On threads. Strung across the path.

Then she saw the Master stepping out of the main hall and bowed. Exactly as deeply as protocol required. Not a finger's width more.

[Baek Ha-seon|matter-of-fact] Sect Leader Seo Hwa-ryeong. I am Baek Ha-seon, Inspector of the Murim Alliance, Seoan branch. A complaint has been lodged against your sect. I am here to examine whether it is justified. I will ask questions. I expect true answers. Anyone who lies to me is liable under Article Fourteen of the statutes. Do you understand?

The Master turned her head so her left ear faced the inspector.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|too loud] I understood half of it. My right ear is broken. Please speak from the left.

Baek Ha-seon blinked. Once.

Then she took a step to the left.

[Baek Ha-seon|louder, matter-of-fact, without the slightest embarrassment] I'll repeat that.

***

She questioned them in the main hall.

Under the sagging roof, before the altar with the ancestral tablets, beside the wooden basin beneath the two missing tiles. Baek Ha-seon sat on a stool Dal had brought, the wooden box on her knees, a brush in her hand, and took notes herself even though the clerk beside her was also writing. Master Pyeon sat against the wall and puffed.

She questioned each of them separately. The Master. Do-yun. Seol-a. Dal. Chil. She asked about Mid-Autumn night. Who had stood where. How many men had come. From where. With what weapons. She asked about the side gate, and Chil turned red and said he had opened it because he wanted to catch a thief, and the inspector wrote it down without changing expression and said only: Thank you. That was honest.

Then she asked about the sound.

[Baek Ha-seon|matter-of-fact, to the Master] Several witnesses from Hacheon report that on that night the whole mountain rang. And that afterward, for a moment, all the bells fell silent at once. Three disciples of the Iron Gate School who were on the road fell from their horses. One vomited. They state that their qi was thrown into confusion for a breath. Is that true?

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|calm] I don't know. I was up in the tower throwing a man off it.

[Baek Ha-seon|without looking up] Who made the mountain ring?

The Master hesitated. Only for a heartbeat.

[Mok Yeon|calm, from the edge of the hall] I did.

Everyone turned to him.

***

Baek Ha-seon looked at him. For a long time. With that gaze that filled a blank page.

[Baek Ha-seon|matter-of-fact] Name. Age. Position.

[Mok Yeon|calm] Mok Yeon. Sixteen. Disciple.

[Master Pyeon|snorting, from the wall] The dog boy! Inspector, that's the one who poured tea on the Righteous Blade's robe. He isn't right in the head.

[Baek Ha-seon|without looking at Pyeon] Master Pyeon, you are here as the complainant, not as a witness. If you speak unbidden again, I will have you removed.

Pyeon shut his mouth. Behind the inspector the clerk wrote very fast with his head down, and Mok Yeon was fairly certain he was grinning.

[Baek Ha-seon|to Mok Yeon] Explain to me what you did.

Mok Yeon explained it.

He explained it honestly, because he couldn't do otherwise, and because he knew she would notice if he didn't. Not because she heard undertones. She didn't have a single tone of the Nine Bells Chant. But because in twenty-three years she would become the best inspector the Alliance had, and because he had watched her in a prison in Mujang listening all night.

He explained the First Tone and the Second and Bell Ear. He explained that two bells make a third tone, and forty-seven little bells make a mountain. He explained the pause. The tenth. The moment in which a bell has been struck and has not yet sounded.

[Mok Yeon|calm] Every person has a rhythm. Their heart. Their breath. Their qi. When everything around them swings in a different rhythm and suddenly falls silent, they lose their own for a moment. Like a dancer when the music stops. They stumble. Nothing more.

[Baek Ha-seon|writing] Nothing more.

[Mok Yeon|honest] Nothing more. It takes nothing. It harms nothing. It only throws what's there into disorder. For one breath.

[Baek Ha-seon|looking up] And you did it on purpose.

[Mok Yeon|calm] Yes. Twenty men came who wanted to kill us. We were five. And a child.

Baek Ha-seon put down her brush.

***

[Baek Ha-seon|matter-of-fact, after a pause] Article Nine of the statutes of the Murim Alliance. A demonic art is an art that disturbs and confuses the qi of other martial artists.

Master Pyeon nodded vigorously against the wall.

[Baek Ha-seon|without pausing] For the purpose of stealing, poisoning, or permanently destroying it. So reads the full text, adopted one hundred and two years ago after the Blood Marrow trials in Mujang. The second half of the sentence tends to be left out of complaints.

Master Pyeon stopped nodding.

Mok Yeon felt his heart skip a beat. Blood Marrow trials. He glanced at the Master. She sat perfectly still, left ear turned to the inspector, her face giving nothing away. But her hand was on the pipe, and the pipe was trembling.

[Baek Ha-seon|calm] So I am examining two things. Whether your art disturbs the qi of others. You have just told me it does. And whether it steals, poisons, or destroys it. That I will determine myself.

She stood. She turned to the Alliance guard, the broad, silent man with the saber on his back.

[Baek Ha-seon|matter-of-fact] Guard Tak. You are first rank. Stand in the tower. Beneath the bells.

Tak looked at her. Then at the tower. Then at Mok Yeon. He said nothing, as he had said nothing so far, but his face said: If this boy does anything to me, I'll break his neck.

[Baek Ha-seon|to Mok Yeon] And you do it again. Not the whole mountain. Just enough for me to see.

***

They all climbed into the tower. Almost all. Dal stayed on the top rung as always, and Master Pyeon stayed below, because the ladder groaned under him.

Guard Tak stood between Dawn and Dew, legs apart, arms folded, with the face of a man who has survived worse. Baek Ha-seon stood at the open side where Hwang Ryeom had stood, the wooden box under her arm.

Mok Yeon laid his hand on Dawn. Seol-a laid hers on Dew. They hadn't agreed on it. She looked at him and nodded, and he knew she would hold the tenth beat without his having to tell her.

He listened until Dawn came to rest.

Then they both pulled.

The tone wasn't loud. It wasn't like Mid-Autumn night. Mok Yeon didn't put everything into it, only enough. Dawn spoke, deep and full, Dew answered, bright, and between them, out of the air, the third tone. It swung through the tower, over their heads, through Guard Tak's ribs.

And then, as the third tone was at its fullest, Mok Yeon and Seol-a held their breath at the same moment. And laid their hands flat on the bronze.

The bells fell silent.

Not slowly. All at once. As if someone had slammed a door.

Guard Tak swayed. Only a little. He unfolded his arms and grabbed the beam beside him, and for a moment his face went blank, like the face of a man walking down a staircase who expects one more step than there is. Then he caught himself. He stood straight again. He looked at Mok Yeon.

[Guard Tak|hoarse, the first word he had spoken] What was that?

***

Baek Ha-seon took her time.

She stepped over to Tak and laid two fingers on his wrist, on the pulse, and then on his throat, and then flat on his lower belly, below the navel, where the dantian lies. Mok Yeon saw that she knew what she was doing. She examined qi like a physician. She took her time. She asked Tak about his breathing and his sight and whether he was in pain, and Tak answered curtly: No. No. No.

[Baek Ha-seon|finally, matter-of-fact] His qi is complete. Nothing is missing. Nothing is poisoned. Nothing is damaged. Note it.

The clerk, standing at the foot of the ladder, wrote.

[Baek Ha-seon|to Mok Yeon, calm] A shield also disturbs the qi of a sword stroke. It throws it into disorder, for a moment. No one calls a shield demonic.

She climbed down the ladder. At the bottom stood Master Pyeon, opening his mouth.

[Baek Ha-seon|without stopping] Your disciples fell off their horses, Master Pyeon, because they are bad riders. I've seen them. One holds his reins like a broom.

Up in the tower, Do-yun made a sound like a strangled ox. Chil bit his fist.

***

She didn't leave until evening. Before she did, she asked one more question.

She asked the Master, at the gate, in the red light of the setting sun, and Mok Yeon stood beside them and heard it.

[Baek Ha-seon|quiet, matter-of-fact] Who attacked you?

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|calm, too loud] I don't know.

Baek Ha-seon looked at her. For a long time. She had no Bell Ear. But she had spent twelve years questioning people who lied.

Then she turned to Mok Yeon.

[Baek Ha-seon|calm] And you?

Mok Yeon looked into her face. Into the long, serious face with the fresh scar through the eyebrow that in twenty-three years would be white and old. He thought of iron bars. Of a whole night. Of a brush writing down every word.

He couldn't lie to her. He hadn't been able to in his first life, and he couldn't now.

[Mok Yeon|quietly] I'm not allowed to tell you that.

Baek Ha-seon looked at him. Something moved in her face. Not anger. Something like interest.

[Baek Ha-seon|after a pause] You don't lie.

[Mok Yeon|calm] No.

[Baek Ha-seon|quietly] But you hide everything.

He said nothing.

She opened the wooden box, took out a brush and a sheet, and wrote something down. Just one line. He didn't see what. Then she shut the box.

[Baek Ha-seon|matter-of-fact, to the Master again] The investigation is not concluded. I will remain in Hacheon. I hear you mean to bring a bell up out of the river. I want to be there. I want to see what your art does when it isn't aimed at murderers.

She bowed, exactly as deeply as in the morning. Then she stopped. She wasn't looking at the Master. She was looking out, over the gate, over the forty-seven steps, to the north.

[Baek Ha-seon|very quietly, so that only Mok Yeon and the Master heard] The complaint came two days after the attack. It was well written. Too well for Master Pyeon. And someone at the Seoan branch asked that I, specifically, be the one to examine it. I don't like being the tool of someone I don't know.

She went down the steps. The clerk and the guard followed her, and Master Pyeon followed them, puffing, with a face like a thundercloud.

Mok Yeon stood at the gate beside the Master and watched them go.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|quietly] Do you know her?

[Mok Yeon|after a pause] She arrested me once. Twenty-three years from now.

The Master looked at him. Then she laughed. Short and rough, and it did him good.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|dry] And?

[Mok Yeon|calm] She listened to me. All night. She was the only one.
