# Chapter 21: The Morning After

The Master insisted on burying the two dead men.

Do-yun objected. It was the first time Mok Yeon had heard him contradict the Master. He sat on a stool outside the kitchen, his shoulder thickly bandaged, his ribs wrapped in linen, and said they should hand the two over to the town watch. The town watch had pits for people without names. The two were murderers. They had wanted to kill Chil, and Seol-a, and everyone else.

The Master didn't hear him. She only half heard him, with her left ear, and so she tilted her head a little to the side when she listened, like a bird. She waited until he was finished.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|too loud, without noticing] They died on my mountain. On my mountain we bury our dead. All of them.

Do-yun fell silent. Then he stood up with a soft groan and fetched the shovel.

***

They buried them behind the furnace, where the nettles had been. Two shallow graves side by side, without stones, without names. Do-yun dug one-handed. Mok Yeon held the lamp, because he couldn't hold anything else with his bandaged hand. Dal brought two bowls of rice and set them on the fresh earth, without a word, the way he set them on the ladder for Chil.

Seol-a stood at the edge and watched. One of the two had been hers.

She hadn't slept that night. She had cleaned her sword three times, four times, until there was no trace left on it, and then she had kept wiping it with a cloth until the Master took it out of her hand. Now she stood there, back straight, looking at the grave, and her face was perfectly calm.

Mok Yeon stood beside her.

[Yu Seol-a|quietly, not looking at him] He was young. Younger than Do-yun. He had a braid, like the farmers in the north. When he came over the wall he looked at me, and he was afraid. I heard it. Beneath everything else, he was afraid.

Mok Yeon said nothing.

[Yu Seol-a|barely audible] You killed many in your first life. Didn't you.

[Mok Yeon|after a long pause] Yes.

[Yu Seol-a|quietly] Does it get easier?

He thought about it. He thought of forty years. Of lantern men in alleys and on mountain paths and in a prison in Mujang. Of faces he had forgotten, and one he couldn't.

[Mok Yeon|honest] No. You only get better at carrying it.

She nodded. Slowly. Then she bent, took a handful of earth from the edge of the grave, scattered it over it the way one does at burials, and walked away.

***

Ui-jin hadn't ridden back to Seoan.

He had spent the night on the steps of the main hall with the torn lantern in his hands, and when the town watch had gone and Madam Ok had left with her porters and Min-ji had released Do-yun only in exchange for a promise to marry her very soon, he was still there. He had watched the burial from a distance and said nothing.

At midday he came to the Master.

He bowed deeply, and the Master turned her head so her left ear faced him.

[Cheon Ui-jin|quiet, very polite] Sect Leader. I want to thank you. For letting me be here. And to ask your forgiveness for not coming sooner. I set out as soon as I knew Seol-a was… that she was gone. I borrowed a horse from the inn on the north road. I would have been here sooner, but it went lame.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|calm, a little too loud] You came. That's enough.

[Cheon Ui-jin|hesitating] May I ask you something?

The Master looked at him. Mok Yeon, standing in the kitchen doorway, heard her heart give a jump.

[Cheon Ui-jin|quietly] The men last night. With the black lanterns. One of them looked at me and said: The son. I heard it. He said it as if he knew me. And then they all left. At once. When I came.

He held up the torn lantern. The black paper fluttered in the wind.

[Cheon Ui-jin|very quietly] Why would bandits know me?

***

The Master didn't answer at once.

Mok Yeon heard what she was thinking. Not in words. In her heartbeat, in her breath, in the way her fingers closed around the pipe she held and didn't light. She was thinking what he was thinking. That this young man stood before them with a heart that had nothing beneath it, and that anything they told him would be in Seoan by tonight. Not because he would betray them. But because he would ask his father. Because he loved his father.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|slowly] Your face is well known, Young Master. Across the whole province. You've traveled with your father through every town. Every bandit between Seoan and Mujang knows who the Righteous Blade's son is. And every bandit knows what happens to him if he harms a hair on your head.

It wasn't a lie. Mok Yeon heard it. Every sentence was true.

Ui-jin heard no undertone. But he looked at the Master for a long time, and then he looked over at Seol-a, who stood at the edge of the training ground and turned her eyes away when he looked at her.

[Cheon Ui-jin|quietly] That's true. I know that.

He said it as if he knew it wasn't all.

***

He found Seol-a in the stable in the afternoon.

Mok Yeon heard it because he was sitting in the tower and because his left ear was slowly coming back. It didn't come back completely. It was as if he heard the world through a cloth, through the whine, which no longer screamed but hummed. But he heard enough. He heard Chestnut snort in his stall when he recognized Ui-jin's footsteps. He heard Seol-a currying him.

He shouldn't have listened. He did anyway.

[Cheon Ui-jin|quietly] He looks good. You rode him well.

[Yu Seol-a|without looking up] He didn't hesitate once. Not at night. Not at the ford at Dongmun.

[Cheon Ui-jin|after a pause] You promised to explain everything to me.

[Yu Seol-a|very quietly] I didn't promise anything.

A long silence. The currycomb scraped over Chestnut's coat.

[Cheon Ui-jin|quiet, almost pleading] Seol-a. You fled my house in the middle of the night. With blood on your hand. Less than three weeks later murderers come up your sect's mountain, and you're standing on the wall with blood on your sword, and when I arrive the murderers say The son and vanish. And now your master tells me about bandits, and everything she says sounds true, and it still isn't all of it. I'm not stupid.

[Yu Seol-a|barely audible] I know you're not stupid.

[Cheon Ui-jin|urgent] Then tell me.

The currycomb stopped scraping.

Mok Yeon heard Seol-a's heart. It was beating fast and loud, like the night she stood on the wall. He heard her open her mouth. He heard the truth behind her teeth, heavy as bronze, the way it had sat on his own tongue on the roof.

He heard her swallow it.

[Yu Seol-a|very quietly] Not yet. Please. I can't. Not yet.

Ui-jin said nothing for a long time. Then Mok Yeon heard footsteps, and a hand stroking Chestnut's nose, and a soft snort.

[Cheon Ui-jin|quietly, to the horse] Stay here, Chestnut. Look after her.

[Yu Seol-a|startled] You're not taking him?

[Cheon Ui-jin|a crooked smile in his voice] I said I'd come for him when you explained it to me. You haven't explained it yet.

***

He rode out in the late afternoon. On the lame horse from the inn, at a walk, because it couldn't go faster.

Mok Yeon stood at the gate as he left. Ui-jin stopped beside him and looked down at him. He looked tired. Older than two days ago.

[Cheon Ui-jin|quietly] You're the one who was ringing in the tower. With the little one. I heard you from below, from the steps. And then… then the whole mountain sang. And for a moment I didn't know which way was up. My horse stumbled.

He shook his head.

[Cheon Ui-jin|quietly] I've never heard anything like it. No one in Seoan has ever heard anything like it. Not even my father.

Mok Yeon looked up at him. He wanted to say something. He didn't know what.

[Cheon Ui-jin|after a pause] I'll tell him about it. My father. About the bandits and the lanterns and the singing mountain. He'll want to know who it was. He'll want to help. He always helps.

[Mok Yeon|very quietly] I know.

Ui-jin looked at him. Something in his face changed. Not suspicion. Something smaller. Like a person standing in a familiar room who notices a piece of furniture has been moved and doesn't know which.

[Cheon Ui-jin|quietly] You say that exactly the way Seol-a does.

Then he rode down the steps, and Mok Yeon watched him until he disappeared at the bend.

***

That night they sat in the tower. All of them. For the first time.

The Master beneath Dawn. Do-yun beneath Dew, leaning against the beam, his arm in a sling. Seol-a at the open side where Hwang Ryeom had stood, knees drawn up. Dal on the top rung of the ladder, because he refused to come all the way up on his bad knees. Chil beside Mok Yeon, so close their shoulders touched.

They had told Chil everything. In the afternoon, after Ui-jin left. The Master had said he had the right. He had opened a gate and rung a bell, and anyone who had done both was no longer a child.

Chil had listened. Mouth open the whole time. At the end he had looked at Mok Yeon and said:

[Bang Chil|awed, whispering] I knew it. You really are a ghost.

Now he sat in the tower saying nothing, which was unusual enough that everyone kept glancing at him.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|too loud] He knows now.

No one asked who.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|continuing] He knows we know. He knows we were expecting him. He knows his son was here and saw the lanterns. He knows a mountain sang and twenty of his men didn't know which way was up. And he doesn't know how.

[Han Do-yun|slowly] Will he come back?

Everyone looked at Mok Yeon.

He sat beneath Dawn and thought. He thought of the first life. In the first life, nothing had come after Lantern Night. There had been nothing left to come for. He had been alone in a black, wet courtyard, and Cheon Mu-gyeong had never thought of the Nine Bells Gate again.

This life was different now. He had walked past the edge of what he knew. From here on he knew nothing. Not next week, not next month. He was as blind as all of them.

[Mok Yeon|honest] I don't know.

It was quiet in the tower. Outside the wind sang in the little bells on the slope.

[Mok Yeon|after a while, slowly] But not like that. Not again with lanterns in the night. He tried it once, and it went wrong, and all of Hacheon heard. If he comes back, he'll come by day. With something that doesn't need a lantern.

[Yu Seol-a|quietly] With what?

Mok Yeon looked at the Master. She looked at him. He knew she was thinking the same thing. A man who had eight hundred sacks of rice burned so he could give them away in the Yun family's name. A man who sent a bell as a gift in order to ask a question. A man who never used a knife when he could use a word.

[Seo Hwa-ryeong|calm] With the law.

***

Later, when the others had gone down, Mok Yeon stayed alone in the tower.

He laid his bandaged hand on Dawn and listened. His left ear hummed. His right heard clearly. Together they heard the world as if through two different windows, and he would have to learn to look through both at once.

He thought of Ui-jin on the lame horse, somewhere on the road to Seoan. Of a young man standing in a familiar room who noticed a piece of furniture had been moved.

In his first life Ui-jin had fallen from his horse at twenty-seven. From Chestnut. On a clear day, on a mountain road.

Chestnut was standing in the Unmu stable now. Snorting in his sleep.

Mok Yeon didn't know whether it meant anything. He didn't know anything anymore. That was the strangest thing about this morning after: for the first time since he woke, the future was as dark to him as it was to anyone else.

He found that it didn't frighten him.

It only made him very, very awake.
