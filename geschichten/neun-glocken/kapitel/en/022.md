# Chapter 22: The Bell in the River

Bang Chil didn't walk past the side gate anymore.

He hadn't decided that. His feet had decided it. If he wanted to get from the woodpile to the kitchen, he went across the courtyard, the long way round, past the main hall, though it was three times as far. If Dal sent him to the well, he went along the back of the furnace. When the wind knocked the deepest little bell against the wood of the side gate at night, tock, tock, he lay on his straw mat with his hands over his ears.

Nobody scolded him. That was the worst part.

The Master could only hear with one ear now. If you spoke to her from the right, she turned her head like a hen and said What?, far too loudly. She laughed about it. She said at least now she didn't have to listen to Dal humming while he cooked. Dal had then hummed louder than ever for three days straight, and the Master had laughed and laughed. Everyone had laughed.

Chil had laughed too. And then he had gone behind the woodpile and thrown up.

***

On the fifth day after Lantern Night he went to the river.

He told Mok Yeon first. He had made up his mind never to go anywhere in secret again. Mok Yeon was sitting in the tower as always, beneath the bell called Dawn, his bandaged hand on his knee, and listened as Chil said he was going fishing. At the river. At Bell Bend.

Mok Yeon looked at him. For a long time. With the look Chil used to find creepy and now only found sad.

[Mok Yeon|calm] Fishing.

[Bang Chil|defiant] Fishing.

[Mok Yeon|after a pause] You don't have a rod.

Chil looked at his empty hands. Then back at Mok Yeon.

[Bang Chil|quietly] I want to find Current.

Mok Yeon said nothing for a long time. Then he nodded.

[Mok Yeon|calm] Be back before midnight. Or I'll come and get you.

***

Bell Bend lay an hour downstream from Hacheon, where the Cheongnyu swung in a wide curve around a rocky outcrop and the water turned deep and dark and slow. Willows stood on the bank, trailing their branches in the water, and on the rock that jutted into the river like a nose sat an old man, fishing.

He was very old. So old his skin looked like willow bark, and so thin the wind could have knocked him over if he hadn't sat so heavily on his rock. He had a bamboo rod and a straw hat with a hole in it, and beside him stood a basket with nothing in it.

Chil knew him. Everyone in Hacheon knew him. Old Gong. He had been sitting at Bell Bend since Dal was young.

[Bang Chil|politely, bowing] Grandfather Gong. Are they biting?

[Old Gong|without turning, cracked] Not for sixty years. I'm not fishing for fish.

Chil sat down beside him on the rock. The stone was warm from the sun. Below, the river flowed brown and slow, and a few charred scraps of paper boats from the Lantern Festival still drifted on the water.

[Bang Chil|after a while] Then what are you fishing for?

Old Gong slowly turned his head. His eyes were white. Not entirely white. Milky, like water someone has sprinkled rice flour into. He didn't look at Chil. He looked past him.

[Old Gong|quietly] You're the little one from Unmu. The one who rang.

[Bang Chil|startled] How do you know…

[Old Gong|cracked, almost smiling] I heard it. Five nights ago. All of Hacheon heard it. Two bells, fast, back and forth. One deep, one high. And the high one wobbled, like someone was pulling it who was too light and hanging off it with his whole body.

Chil looked at his hands. The palms were still red where the rope had torn the skin.

[Old Gong|quietly] And then the river answered.

***

He told it as the sun went down.

Sixty years ago, when he was a young man and could still see, the Fifth Master of the Nine Bells Gate had sold a bell. The third. Current. To a merchant in Seoan who meant to melt it down. They had been bad years; a flood had washed away the fields around Hacheon, and the sect had nothing to eat. So they took Current down from the tower and loaded it onto a barge to carry it upriver to Seoan.

[Old Gong|quietly, telling it] The bargemen drank. It was winter, it was cold, they had rice wine. And here, at the bend, where the water turns, they swung too far out. The barge tipped onto its side, very slowly. The bell slid. It snapped the rope that held it. And it fell.

He was quiet for a while. Below, the water swirled around the rock with a soft gurgling sound.

[Old Gong|cracked] I was on the bank. I saw it fall. It rang when it hit the water. Once. And then it sank, and underwater it kept ringing, quieter and quieter, until you couldn't hear anything.

[Bang Chil|whispering] And since then?

[Old Gong|calm] Since then I've sat here. People say you can hear it at night. For sixty years I haven't heard it. I thought it was dead. Bells can die, you know. If no one strikes them, they forget how they sound.

He turned his milky face toward the river.

[Old Gong|very quietly] But five nights ago, when your bells were screaming, I was sitting here. And the water trembled. I felt it in my feet, through the stone.

***

Chil stayed sitting when the sun went down. He stayed when Old Gong reeled in his line, which had no hook on it, and tapped his way slowly home along the bank. He stayed when the moon rose, no longer quite full, and the willows went black and the river silver.

He heard nothing.

He tried. He tried the way Mok Yeon had shown him during morning stance. Nine and one. He managed the nine, mostly. He never managed the one. He heard his own heartbeat and the river and an owl in the willows, and nothing else.

He thought of the Master, who had to turn her head like a hen now.

He thought of the side gate. Of the handle of the new bolt under his hand. Turn first, then pull. He had turned. He had pulled. He had been so proud.

He didn't cry. He had cried enough. He only sat there looking at the water, wishing he could fall into it himself like the bell and lie at the bottom where it was dark and still.

Then he heard footsteps behind him.

He heard them because they weren't quiet. Someone was walking over the stones on the bank, slowly, a little unevenly, like someone who hears badly on one side and so sometimes turns his head.

[Mok Yeon|calm, behind him] It's not midnight. But almost.

Chil didn't turn around.

Mok Yeon sat down beside him on the rock. Where Old Gong had sat. He had his bandaged hand on his knee and his head turned a little, because his left ear was still humming.

For a long time they said nothing.

[Bang Chil|finally, quietly] I didn't hear it.

[Mok Yeon|nodding] I know.

[Bang Chil|barely audible] I wanted to bring it back. For the Master. Because I broke her ear. A bell for an ear. I thought that was fair.

Mok Yeon looked at him sideways.

[Mok Yeon|calm] You didn't break her ear. She did that herself. She chose it. She put her cheek against the bell and knew what would happen.

[Bang Chil|bitter] Because I opened the gate.

[Mok Yeon|after a pause] Yes. That too.

Chil looked at him. He had expected Mok Yeon to say no. Everyone said no. The Master, Do-yun, Seol-a, Dal. It's not your fault, Chil. You didn't know, Chil. Everyone said it, and no one meant it the way Chil needed.

Mok Yeon said yes.

And it was strange. It hurt less than the no.

***

[Bang Chil|after a long while, quietly] What was I like? In the first life. Before. Before…

Mok Yeon looked at the river. Chil saw his jaw working.

[Mok Yeon|very quietly] I don't know. I didn't pay attention. I was sixteen and afraid of everything and didn't look at anyone. I don't know if you put offerings on ladders back then. I don't know if you could do the Sparrow Step. I remember nothing about you. Only a sound. Very brief.

Chil swallowed.

[Mok Yeon|continuing, calm] For forty-one years that was all I had of you. A sound. I heard it every night. And now…

He turned his head and looked at Chil.

[Mok Yeon|calm] Now I have rice balls in cabbage leaves. And a mole that moves up when you grin. And someone who shouts bell ghost when he's scared. And someone who holds on to a rope until his hands bleed while a murderer stands beside him.

He looked at the river again.

[Mok Yeon|quietly] You opened a gate, Chil. But you've given me a thousand sounds for it. I wouldn't trade.

Chil couldn't answer. He sat there looking at the silver river and felt something in his chest that had been knotted tight for five days loosen a tiny bit. Not completely. Just a bit. Enough to breathe.

***

[Mok Yeon|after a while, offhand] It's nearly midnight.

[Bang Chil|confused] So?

[Mok Yeon|calm] I asked Do-yun and Seol-a to ring at midnight. Both bells. Once.

Chil stared at him.

[Mok Yeon|calm] Old Gong said the river answered when we rang. A bell doesn't answer by itself. It answers when someone calls. You sat here waiting for it to call you. But you have to call it.

Chil wanted to say something. He didn't get the chance.

From Unmu, an hour upriver, over the black hills and the silver fields, came a sound.

Dawn. Deep and full. And Dew, bright, like water falling from a leaf. And between them, out of the air, the third tone.

It was faint, so far away. A breath. Chil heard it more with his chest than with his ears.

[Mok Yeon|very quietly] Put your hand on the stone.

Chil put his hand on the rock. The warm, smooth rock Old Gong had sat on for sixty years.

And beneath his hand the stone trembled.

Very finely. Like a breath. Like the bell called Dawn under Mok Yeon's hand when it came to rest. It didn't come from the rock. It came from deeper. From beneath the rock. From beneath the water.

Chil looked at the river.

In the middle of the bend, where the water was deepest and darkest, a circle formed on the surface. No wind. No fish. A ring spreading slowly, silver in the moonlight. And then a second inside it. And a third. As if someone had thrown a stone into a pond. Except no one had thrown a stone.

And Chil heard it.

He didn't hear it with his ears. He heard it with the hand on the stone, and with his belly, and with the small thread of qi stirring beneath his navel, the way Mok Yeon always described it during morning stance and the way he had never once felt it. A hum. Deep. Muffled. Like someone singing under a thick blanket.

Current was answering.

[Bang Chil|whispering, stunned] It's alive.

[Mok Yeon|quietly, eyes closed] It's alive.

Chil noticed he was breathing. Nine times. Without meaning to, without counting. And on the tenth he held his breath, because he was afraid of scaring the hum away. He waited in the pause. In the moment when a bell has been struck and hasn't sounded yet.

And in that pause he heard his own heart, and the hum beneath the water, and both beat in the same rhythm.

Then it was over. The rings on the water faded out. The stone under his hand went still.

Chil sat there looking at his hand.

[Mok Yeon|quietly, almost smiling] That was the Second Tone, Chil. Breath and qi swinging together. You just found it.

***

They walked home together in the moonlight along the river. Chil talked the whole way. About the bell, about Old Gong, about the rings on the water, about how they would bring Current up, with boats and ropes and the tofu seller's ox, even though the ox wouldn't climb steps, but maybe you could hold radishes in front of it. Mok Yeon listened and said almost nothing.

At the Hacheon market, as the sun was already rising, Chil stopped.

On the wall of notices, where three months ago the Righteous Blade's speech had been announced, hung a new paper. Large. With a red seal at the bottom right that Chil had never seen. Two crossed swords in a circle.

Chil could read better than Do-yun. He read it aloud, slowly, character by character.

[Bang Chil|reading, haltingly] On the complaint… of the Iron Gate School… and other honorable houses… the Nine Bells Gate on Unmu is under investigation… on suspicion of practicing a demonic art… that disturbs and confuses the qi of other martial artists… Inspector Baek Ha-seon of the Murim Alliance will arrive in Hacheon on the first day of the tenth month…

He stopped reading. He turned to Mok Yeon.

Mok Yeon stood very still, looking at the red seal.

[Bang Chil|quietly, confused] What does demonic mean?

[Mok Yeon|flat] It means he found a word.
